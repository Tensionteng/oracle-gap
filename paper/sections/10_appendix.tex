\section{Proofs and Derivations}
\label{app:proofs}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:oracle}.}
By orthogonality of the conditional mean, $\mathbb{E}\|y - m\|^2 = \mathbb{E}\|y - \mu + \mu - m\|^2 = \mathbb{E}\|\mu - m\|^2 + \mathbb{E}\,\mathrm{tr}\,\Sigma(x)$, since $\mathbb{E}[\langle \varepsilon, \mu - m \rangle] = 0$. The optimal forecast satisfies $\mathbb{E}\|\hat{y}^\star - m\|^2 = \mathbb{E}\|\mu - m\|^2$, which is strictly smaller whenever unpredictable variance exists. The amplitude-ratio bound follows from $\mathrm{Var}(y) = \mathrm{Var}(\mu) + \bar{\sigma}^2$ and $\mathrm{std}(\hat{y}^\star)^2 \le \mathrm{Var}(\mu)$.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:interval}.}
Expand $\mathbb{E}\|\hat{y} + \delta - y\|^2 = \mathbb{E}\|\hat{y} - y\|^2 + 2\langle \delta, \mathbb{E}[\hat{y} - y] \rangle + |\delta|^2$. Since $\mathbb{E}[\hat{y} - y \mid x] = -b(x)$, the cross term equals $-2\langle \delta, b\rangle$, giving $\Delta\mathcal{L} = |\delta|^2 - 2\langle\delta, b\rangle$. The right side is negative exactly when $|\delta| < 2|b|\cos\theta$, and minimized at $\delta^\star = b$.

\paragraph{Derivation of the Gaussian fixed point \eqref{eq:fixedpoint}.}
With $w(y) = 1 + \alpha\mathbf{1}[y > c]$, the first-order condition $c = \mathbb{E}[w(y)\,y] / \mathbb{E}[w(y)]$ gives
\begin{equation}
    c = \frac{\mu + \alpha\left(\mu\,\bar{\Phi}(t) + \sigma\varphi(t)\right)}{1 + \alpha\,\bar{\Phi}(t)},
    \qquad t = \frac{c - \mu}{\sigma},
\end{equation}
using the truncated normal moments $\mathbb{E}[y \mid y > c] = \mu + \sigma\varphi(t)/\bar{\Phi}(t)$. At $t = 0$, the right-hand side exceeds $\mu$ by $\alpha\sigma\varphi(0)/(1 + \alpha/2)$, and a first-order expansion of the fixed point gives \eqref{eq:fixedpoint}. The $0.4\alpha\sigma$ form quoted in the main text is the small-$\alpha$ slope of this fixed point; the exact shift at $\alpha = 1$ is $0.27\sigma$, so the quoted form is an upper bound on the correction magnitude for $\alpha \le 1$. When the anchor equals $\mu$, the objective is symmetric about $\mu$ and develops minima at $\mu \pm \delta(\alpha)$, a double-well bifurcation that explains the anchor's role in controlling the correction's symmetry.

\section{Diagnostic Protocol Details}
\label{app:diagnostic}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
All reported gaps use the following fixed protocol unless stated otherwise. Statistics are computed per window and per channel around the context-mean anchor, in the per-window, per-channel standardized space, so that series of different scales contribute comparably and residual permutation remains meaningful. The evaluation set is the last $96{+}96$ steps of each series (the tail protocol), with twenty bootstrap repetitions of the window permutation; the mean is reported with a ninety-five percent confidence interval from the bootstrap distribution. All confidence intervals in the audit are below $\pm 0.005$.

\paragraph{Window-set sensitivity.}
The gap is a property of the evaluated window set, not only of the dataset-model pair, and we therefore fix one protocol for all cross-model comparisons. Table~\ref{tab:windows} illustrates the sensitivity: on SZ\_TAXI, a sliding protocol that matches the TSLib test split places PatchTST near zero, while the tail protocol reveals strong over-dispersion; on solar, the two protocols move the estimate by a similar margin in the opposite direction. The law in Section~\ref{sec:law} was verified within each fixed protocol separately, so protocol sensitivity does not affect its validity, but absolute gap values across protocols should not be compared.

\paragraph{Anchor.}
The context-mean anchor is used throughout. It coincides with the reference implemented by reversible instance normalization, which keeps the under-shoot region aligned with the shrinkage attractor of normalized models. A seasonal-naive anchor defines a different notion of shrinkage and is left to future work; Section~\ref{sec:regionfocal} shows the analogous choice inside the repair loss matters similarly (the seasonal anchor removes the benefit there).

\paragraph{Variance of the estimator.}
Write $a_i = \mathrm{std}(\hat{y}_i)/\mathrm{std}(\tilde{y}_i)$ for the per-window amplitude ratio of the model against a resampled realization and $b_i$ for the ratio against the true future. Treating the windows as independent, the gap estimator $\hat{g} = \bar{a} - \bar{b}$ has variance $\mathrm{Var}(\hat{g}) \le 2\sigma_a^2 / N$ up to window correlations, and $R$ bootstrap repetitions reduce the resampling contribution by a further factor of $R$. With $N \ge 500$ windows and the per-window dispersion $\sigma_a \approx 0.1$ observed in practice, the resulting ninety-five percent interval is at most $\pm 0.01$, consistent with the intervals below $\pm 0.005$ measured across the audit. The estimator is therefore precise enough to resolve gaps of order $0.01$, an order of magnitude smaller than the effects the law uses.

\section{What Does Not Work: Negative Results and Mechanism Analysis}
\label{app:negative}

We report the negative results that shaped the design, since each rules out a mechanism one might otherwise expect to work.

\paragraph{Conditioning the forecast on predicted structure.}
Injecting the descriptor head's representation into the forecast head, by concatenation or FiLM modulation, does not outperform the parallel auxiliary head. Intervention analysis shows the injection is used: zeroing the descriptor embedding degrades test MSE by up to $0.13$. But permuting it across windows costs an order of magnitude less, so the main path consumes distribution-level calibration rather than window-specific content.

\paragraph{Per-window gating of the repair.}
Modulating the \rfc~coefficient by predicted change-point probability does not beat the static coefficient on Electricity ($0.1784$ versus $0.1758$ MSE), and a frozen random gate matches it ($0.1752$). Per-window gating by the learned descriptor is not the active ingredient; the static counter-bias already captures the available benefit.

\paragraph{Scaling the descriptor objective.}
Fine-tuning Chronos-Bolt with descriptor supervision is neutral on ETTh1 and degrades ETTm1 by $4\%$, and the zero-shot oracle gap is flat from 8M to 311M parameters (Section~\ref{sec:tsfmmap}). We find no scale at which structural supervision improves point accuracy; following \citet{gloeckle2024mtp}, any such effect likely lives in the large-scale fine-tuning regime.

\paragraph{Architecture of the backbone.}
We conjectured that channel-independent backbones would benefit from region weighting while channel-mixing ones would not. DLinear falsifies the conjecture: it is channel-independent, has a near-zero gap on Electricity, and does not benefit. Across five backbones on Electricity (Table~\ref{tab:backbone}), the per-backbone gap orders the outcomes exactly, and models with non-positive gaps are harmed in proportion. The repair's benefit is a function of leftover structure, not architecture family.

\paragraph{Conditional conformal calibration.}
Conditioning split-conformal interval width on the predicted volatility bin or change-point probability leaves the width at equal marginal coverage within $0.3\%$ of the unconditional baseline on ETTh1 and ETTm1. The binned descriptor signal carries no extractable width information at this resolution, consistent with the weak volatility ranking below. Selective prediction by ranking, rather than width modulation, is the effective decision use.

\paragraph{Volatility-signal repair.}
Re-labeling the volatility target in log space does not improve the predicted volatility ranking: the risk-coverage effect at coverage $0.8$ moves from $+3.1\%$ to $+1.9\%$ on ETTh1 and from $+2.3\%$ to $+1.0\%$ on ETTm1, still worse than random. Replacing the binned classification target with a continuous log-volatility regression finally turns the signal usable on ETTm1 ($-1.7\%$ at coverage $0.8$, Spearman $+0.03$), but all four variants fail on ETTh1, where the volatility component of the error is not recoverable from the pooled representation. The change-point channel therefore remains the primary decision signal, and volatility remains an open problem except in the continuous form on ETTm1.

\section{Implementation and Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

\paragraph{Forecasting protocol.}
Task models are trained with the standard TSLib long-term-forecasting protocol: ninety-six input steps, prediction horizons of $96/192/336/720$, train/validation/test splits and normalization by train-split statistics, Adam with the canonical per-model learning rates of the benchmark, batch size $32$ (ETT, solar, SWaT) or $16$ (Electricity, Traffic, SZ\_TAXI), at most ten epochs with early stopping at patience three on the validation task loss, and MSE as the task criterion unless stated otherwise. Backbone configurations follow the canonical benchmark scripts: PatchTST with one encoder layer and two heads on ETT, two layers on Electricity and Traffic; iTransformer with two layers and width $128$ on ETT, three layers and width $512$ on Electricity; DLinear, TimeMixer, and TimesNet with their canonical settings. Reported test MSE and MAE are computed in the normalized space.

\paragraph{Descriptor head.}
The head is a two-layer MLP with hidden width $128$ and six readouts (change-point logit, change-point position, three five-way ordinal classifiers, and a five-dimensional spectral regressor), $21{,}526$ parameters in total, attached to the mean-pooled last hidden state. Labels are computed from each future window: change-point probability and position from a scale-free CUSUM statistic over the first $24$ steps, mapped through a fixed calibrated sigmoid; ordinal bins of drift, volatility, and slope from train-split quantiles at $10/30/70/90$ percent; and spectral centroid, entropy, and three band-energy fractions, z-scored with train-split statistics. The auxiliary loss is an unweighted sum of one binary cross-entropy, one smooth-L1, three cross-entropies, and one squared-error term, scaled by a single coefficient ($0.1$ unless stated otherwise). Validation and early stopping use the task loss only.

\paragraph{RegionFocal.}
The loss uses temperatures $\tau_1 = \tau_2 = 0.1$, context-mean anchor, detached weights, no over-shoot penalty, and a wrong-side weight of half the under-shoot weight unless stated otherwise. The coefficient $\alpha$ is swept in $\{0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4\}$ on development runs; main results use $\alpha = 1$ (Electricity, bizitobs) and $\alpha = 0.5$ (Traffic).

\paragraph{Foundation-model fine-tuning.}
Chronos-Bolt-small is fine-tuned with AdamW (learning rate $10^{-5}$, weight decay $0.01$, gradient clipping $1.0$), batch size $32$, up to ten epochs with patience three, bf16 autocast, and the native nine-quantile pinball loss. The descriptor head attaches to the mask-pooled encoder hidden state (six patch tokens plus the register token). Evaluation follows the official quantile-expansion rollout for horizons beyond sixty-four steps.

\paragraph{Diagnostic.}
The oracle gap is computed with twenty bootstrap repetitions; see Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostic} for the full protocol. Selective-prediction curves are computed on the test split with twenty-five coverage levels; the trained error-prediction baseline uses ridge and two-layer MLP regressors on ten context statistics, fit on the validation split.

\paragraph{Compute and seeds.}
All experiments run on NVIDIA A800 80GB GPUs, one training run per GPU. Main results are reported across seeds $2021/2022/2023$; single-seed cells use seed $2021$. In total, the evaluation comprises more than two hundred task-model training runs and a six-hundred-sixty-five-cell zero-shot audit. Code, the full audit matrix, and the experiment scripts are released in the accompanying repository.

\paragraph{HCAN porting notes.}
Our within-protocol HCAN control attaches the published hierarchical classification component (coarse and fine binning, evidence-theory classifier, hierarchical consistency loss, and hierarchy-aware attention fusion) to the same PatchTST hidden states used by our descriptor head, rather than to the Informer decoder outputs of the original implementation. We also correct a silent view-permutation in the official code that exchanges time and channel indices when the prediction length differs from the channel count. These choices are required to run the component on modern backbones, and they are the reason we treat the port as a within-protocol control only and rely on the published numbers for the original comparison.
