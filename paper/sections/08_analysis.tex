\section{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our results support a two-stage practice. Compute the oracle gap on the target dataset-model pair; a positive gap licenses repair with a bounded counter-bias such as \rfc, while a non-positive gap predicts harm and calls for decision-time signals such as descriptor-driven abstention. The diagnostic is cheap enough to run as a standard preprocessing step.

Several natural mechanisms for exploiting future-structure information turn out not to work, including conditioning the forecast on the predicted structure, per-window gating of the repair by the predicted change-point probability, and scaling the descriptor objective to foundation-model fine-tuning. Appendix~\ref{app:negative} reports these negative results and the mechanism analyses that explain them.

Limitations bound the claims. Positive repair evidence concentrates on datasets with positive gaps, and our backbone coverage, while spanning five families, is not exhaustive. The Gaussian analysis assumes symmetric noise, and the anchor adds a design degree of freedom we have only partially explored. The descriptor head's volatility output is too weak to use, although oracle rankings show it carries the largest selective-prediction signal. All experiments are in the point-forecasting regime; extending the diagnostic and repair to distributional forecasting, where proper scoring rules reward amplitude fidelity directly, is the natural next step.

Over-smoothing is not one phenomenon but two. Rational shrinkage is the price of honest uncertainty, and unexploited structure is the repairable part. The oracle gap tells them apart cheaply and predicts, without exception in our evaluation, when anti-smoothing pays. Where it pays, a region-weighted loss recovers part of the structure; where it does not, a descriptor head reports when the forecast deserves trust. We hope the diagnostic becomes a standard instrument, and that future benchmarks report not only accuracy but the structure that remains to be exploited.
