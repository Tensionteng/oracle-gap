\section{The Oracle Gap Diagnostic}
\label{sec:diagnostic}

Proposition~\ref{prop:oracle} implies that a trained model's amplitude signature is meaningful only relative to the signature an oracle would produce on the same data. We cannot observe the oracle, but we can simulate how the model's own predictions would score as one. Let $\{\hat{y}_i, y_i\}_{i=1}^{N}$ be test-window forecasts and realizations, and let $r_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i$ be the residual windows. If the model captured all predictable structure, residuals would be exchangeable across windows, and $\hat{y}_i$ would act as the conditional mean of a fresh realization $\tilde{y}_i = \hat{y}_i + r_{\pi(i)}$ for a random permutation $\pi$. We define
\begin{equation}
    \gap \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{\pi}\!\left[\frac{\mathrm{std}(\hat{y})}{\mathrm{std}(\tilde{y})}\right] \;-\; \frac{\mathrm{std}(\hat{y})}{\mathrm{std}(y)},
    \label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
where standard deviations are computed per window and channel around the context-mean anchor and then pooled. The first term is the amplitude ratio the model would exhibit as an oracle, the second the observed ratio. A positive gap means the model under-shoots more against the true future than against realizations consistent with its own residuals---the residuals still contain window-specific, non-exchangeable structure; a gap near zero means the residuals behave like noise and the shrinkage is rational.

\paragraph{Protocol and robustness.}
We compute \eqref{eq:gap} with twenty bootstrap repetitions (confidence intervals below $\pm 0.005$ throughout), after per-window, per-channel standardization so large-scale series do not dominate, on a fixed tail protocol (the last $96{+}96$ steps of each series) because the gap is sensitive to the window set (sensitivity in Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostic}).

The simulation uses the model's own predictions as a proxy for the conditional mean, which makes the diagnostic one-sidedly conservative: if the model is under-dispersed relative to the true conditional mean, the simulated oracle inherits the under-dispersion, so the true gap can only be larger. The estimate is thus a lower bound on repairable structure; a residual that is structured but wrong would evade detection, and the 665-cell audit together with fourteen of fifteen consistent intervention cells bounds that risk empirically.

\paragraph{Reading the gap.}
Three signatures recur: positive gaps (Electricity $+0.030$, Traffic $+0.027$) mark residuals with leftover structure and predict that anti-smoothing interventions help; near-zero gaps (all four ETT series, $-0.02$ to $-0.04$; SWaT $+0.006$) mark rational shrinkage and predict neutral or harmful interventions; and strongly negative gaps (Jena weather $-0.058$) mark models already over-dispersed relative to their residual oracle, where amplitude pressure accelerates the harm. These predictions hold in all but one cell we tested (Section~\ref{sec:law}), and the exception is diagnosable in advance from the prediction geometry. Under window-level exchangeability of residuals, the permutation estimate is a consistent test of residual non-exchangeability (Appendix~\ref{app:diagnostic}); we use it as a signed magnitude, and the law is the empirical warrant for that stronger use.
