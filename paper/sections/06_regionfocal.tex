\section{\rfc: Conditional Repair of Over-Smoothing}
\label{sec:regionfocal}

\subsection{Method}

Let $m$ be a per-channel anchor computed from the context window, for example its mean. For each point $(t, c)$ of the forecast, write $d = y - m$ for the realized deviation and $p = \hat{y} - m$ for the predicted deviation. The point is \emph{under-shooting} when $p$ and $d$ agree in sign and $|p| < |d|$, \emph{over-shooting} when they agree and $|p| > |d|$, and \emph{wrong-sided} when they disagree. \rfc~reweights the squared error by membership in the under-shoot region:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\rfc} = \frac{1}{HC}\sum_{t,c} w_{tc}\,(\hat{y}_{tc} - y_{tc})^2,
    \qquad
    w_{tc} = 1 + \alpha\,\underbrace{\sigma\!\Big(\frac{p_{tc} d_{tc}}{\tau_1}\Big)\,\sigma\!\Big(\frac{1 - r_{tc}}{\tau_2}\Big)}_{\text{soft under-shoot membership}},
    \label{eq:rf}
\end{equation}
where $r_{tc} = |p_{tc}| / (|d_{tc}| + \epsilon)$, $\sigma$ is the logistic function, $\tau_1, \tau_2 = 0.1$, and the weights are detached from the gradient graph. Three properties are load-bearing. The zero-loss set is unchanged: \eqref{eq:rf} vanishes exactly at $\hat{y} = y$, so the loss introduces no spurious targets. The fixed-point shift is bounded by Proposition~\ref{prop:interval} and the Gaussian analysis~\eqref{eq:fixedpoint}, at about $0.4\alpha\sigma$ per window. And over-shooting is never penalized, so the correction pushes only against shrinkage.

Table~\ref{tab:main} shows the main result. On Electricity, which has the largest oracle gap among our task-model datasets, \rfc~improves MSE by about 2.3\% across three seeds, and the gain is stable from 96 to 720 prediction steps, in contrast with auxiliary-objective gains that decay with horizon in our experiments. On Traffic the gain is $-0.9\%$ at $\alpha{=}0.5$, and on bizitobs\_application it is $-1.7\%$. The dose-response curve is monotone-improving up to $\alpha{=}1$ and flat beyond, matching the bounded-shift analysis. On datasets with non-positive gap, including SZ\_TAXI under PatchTST ($-0.010$), the same configuration is neutral to mildly negative, as the law requires. The SZ\_TAXI cell is instructive: zero-shot foundation models show the largest positive gap there ($+0.072$), yet PatchTST's own gap is negative, and indeed the intervention harms PatchTST slightly, confirming that the gap is a property of the dataset-model pair rather than the dataset alone.

\paragraph{Behavioral confirmation.}

The repair acts on the intended mechanism. On Electricity, \rfc~reduces the under-shoot rate from 0.63 to 0.57 and raises the amplitude ratio from 0.87 to 0.91, while the wrong-side rate is unchanged. The loss therefore removes part of the unexploited structure rather than inflating variance indiscriminately.

\paragraph{Ablations.}

Ablations on Electricity isolate the design choices. The context-mean and zero anchors give indistinguishable results ($-2.3\%$ versus $-2.2\%$), while a seasonal-naive anchor removes the benefit ($+0.9\%$), so the anchor defines the correct shrinkage reference and is not a free choice. Hard gating matches soft gating ($-2.6\%$ versus $-2.3\%$), and additionally weighting the wrong-side region leaves results unchanged ($-2.4\%$), so the benefit comes specifically from the under-shoot region. We also compare with an HCAN-style hierarchical classification auxiliary component \citep{hcan} under the same backbone and protocol. Our faithful port of the published component does not improve the baseline on Electricity (MSE $0.197$ versus $0.180$); Appendix~\ref{app:repro} documents porting choices that differ from the original Informer-based implementation, and we therefore rely on the authors' published protocol for cross-paper comparison and report our port only as a within-protocol control.

\paragraph{Where the repair does not help.}

The descriptor-gated variant, which modulates $\alpha$ per window by predicted change-point probability, does not improve over the static weighting on Electricity ($0.1784$ versus $0.1758$ MSE), and a randomly initialized gate matches the static version ($0.1752$). Per-window gating by the learned descriptor is thus not the active ingredient; the static counter-bias already captures the available benefit. We return to this in Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.
