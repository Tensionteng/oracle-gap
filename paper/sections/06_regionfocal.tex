\section{\rfc: Conditional Repair of Over-Smoothing}
\label{sec:regionfocal}

\subsection{Method}

Let $m$ be a per-channel anchor computed from the context window, for example its mean. For each point $(t, c)$ of the forecast, write $d = y - m$ for the realized deviation and $p = \hat{y} - m$ for the predicted deviation. The point is \emph{under-shooting} when $p$ and $d$ agree in sign and $|p| < |d|$, \emph{over-shooting} when they agree and $|p| > |d|$, and \emph{wrong-sided} when they disagree. \rfc~reweights the squared error by membership in the under-shoot region:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\rfc} = \frac{1}{HC}\sum_{t,c} w_{tc}\,(\hat{y}_{tc} - y_{tc})^2,
    \qquad
    w_{tc} = 1 + \alpha\,\underbrace{\sigma\!\Big(\frac{p_{tc} d_{tc}}{\tau_1}\Big)\,\sigma\!\Big(\frac{1 - r_{tc}}{\tau_2}\Big)}_{\text{soft under-shoot membership}},
    \label{eq:rf}
\end{equation}
where $r_{tc} = |p_{tc}| / (|d_{tc}| + \epsilon)$, $\sigma$ is the logistic function, $\tau_1, \tau_2 = 0.1$, and the weights are detached from the gradient graph. Three properties are load-bearing. The zero-loss set is unchanged: \eqref{eq:rf} vanishes exactly at $\hat{y} = y$, so the loss introduces no spurious targets. The fixed-point shift is bounded by Proposition~\ref{prop:interval} and the Gaussian analysis~\eqref{eq:fixedpoint}, at about $0.4\alpha\sigma$ per window. And over-shooting is never penalized, so the correction pushes only against shrinkage.

Table~\ref{tab:main} shows the main result. On Electricity, which has the largest oracle gap among our task-model datasets, \rfc~improves MSE by about 2.3\% across three seeds, stable from 96 to 720 prediction steps---in contrast with auxiliary-objective gains that decay with horizon. On Traffic the gain is $-1.0 \pm 0.1\%$ and on bizitobs\_application $-1.4\%$ across three seeds. The dose-response curve is monotone-improving up to $\alpha{=}1$ and flat beyond, matching the bounded-shift analysis. On non-positive-gap datasets, including SZ\_TAXI under PatchTST ($-0.010$), the same configuration is neutral to mildly negative, as the law requires. The SZ\_TAXI cell is instructive: zero-shot foundation models show the largest positive gap there ($+0.072$), yet PatchTST's own gap is negative, and the intervention indeed harms PatchTST slightly, confirming that the gap is a property of the dataset--model pair rather than the dataset alone.

\paragraph{Foundation models after fine-tuning.}
A natural next target is the large zero-shot gaps left by foundation models. Fine-tuning Chronos-Bolt closes its own gap (from $+0.072$ zero-shot to $-0.016$ on SZ\_TAXI), and applying \rfc~to the fine-tuned model degrades accuracy, as the law requires once the gap closes ($+4.6\%$; solar: gap $-0.025$, $+5.2\%$).

\paragraph{Behavioral confirmation.} On Electricity, \rfc~reduces the under-shoot rate from 0.63 to 0.57 and raises the amplitude ratio from 0.87 to 0.91, while the wrong-side rate is unchanged: the repair removes unexploited structure rather than inflating variance.

\paragraph{Ablations.}

Ablations on Electricity isolate the design choices. The context-mean and zero anchors give indistinguishable results ($-2.3\%$ versus $-2.2\%$), while a seasonal-naive anchor removes the benefit ($+0.9\%$), so the anchor defines the correct shrinkage reference and is not a free choice. Hard gating matches soft gating ($-2.6\%$ versus $-2.3\%$), and additionally weighting the wrong-side region leaves results unchanged ($-2.4\%$), so the benefit comes specifically from the under-shoot region. Two control families bound the design from below. Robust losses, the standard off-the-shelf alternative, do not reproduce the effect on this dispersion cell---Huber neutral ($+0.1\%$), MAE harmful ($+2.2\%$), against a three-seed baseline spread below $\pm 0.15\%$---as theory expects: under near-symmetric residuals the conditional median they target coincides with the mean. Post-hoc recalibration fit on validation (global, per-channel, per-step, and per-step-affine gains) recovers at most two-thirds of the benefit ($-1.6\%$) on Electricity and degrades the baseline on m4\_weekly (best variant $+3.1\%$): the repair must act at training time, conditioned per window (artifacts in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). We also compare with an HCAN-style hierarchical classification auxiliary component \citep{hcan} under the same backbone and protocol. Our faithful port of the published component does not improve the baseline on Electricity in any of three seeds (MSE $0.197$/$0.198$/$0.197$ versus $0.180$); given the porting choices required (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}), we rely on the authors' published protocol for cross-paper comparison and report the port as a within-protocol control.

\paragraph{Structure types: lag that context cannot fix.}
Three further datasets probe the boundary of the law. On m4\_weekly and us\_births, \rfc~improves MSE by $5.3\%$ on average across three seeds ($-5.0$/$-5.3$/$-5.5\%$) and by $4.6\%$ on average across three seeds with high variance ($-3.1$/$+1.9$/$-14.6\%$, owing to the small weekly test set), although the dispersion-based oracle gap is negative in both ($-0.026$ and $-0.075$). Residual analysis shows the dominant bias there is level lag (signed residual effect size $+0.11$ and $+0.31$, versus below $0.04$ on Electricity and ETT), which the region weight corrects as Proposition~\ref{prop:interval} requires. A further probe separates repairable lag from model failure: context-to-lag regression yields negative $R^2$ on all improving cells (m4\_weekly $-9.7$, us\_births $-0.3$, Electricity $-1.4$) and a positive $R^2$ only on saugeenday ($+0.35$), the cell that does not improve. The complementary information-injection fix confirms the reading (post-hoc lag correction reaches $-13.2\%$ on saugeenday; Appendix~\ref{app:matrix}, Table~\ref{tab:lagcells}). The repairable regime is thus systematic shrinkage that is not predictable from the context; a context-predictable lag instead calls for information injection (Appendix~\ref{app:matrix}).

\paragraph{Cross-backbone transfer and the median-loss alternative.}
The effect is not specific to PatchTST: under the identical configuration on m4\_weekly, FreTS, a frequency-domain MLP, improves by $6.6 \pm 0.3\%$ across three seeds, while TimesNet shows no consistent effect ($+5.3 \pm 8.9\%$; Appendix~\ref{app:negative}): repairability is conferred by the dataset--model pair, not the dataset or architecture family alone. On lag cells the region weight is not the only effective repair: MAE training, targeting the conditional median rather than the mean, improves m4\_weekly by $6.7 \pm 0.6\%$ across three seeds, slightly exceeding \rfc's $-5.3\%$ (Huber: $-2.3 \pm 0.1\%$); the same losses are neutral to harmful on dispersion cells, where the conditional median coincides with the mean. The practical division of labor is therefore: a median-targeted loss for lag-dominated bias, and \rfc~for the dispersion regime, where neither robust losses nor post-hoc calibration reach it.

\paragraph{Boundary of the lag branch.}
Beyond context-predictability, the lag repair presumes a near-optimal backbone: TimesNet--m4\_weekly, the most misspecified cell we tested, fails through checkpoint selection, with the repaired model's validation gain failing to transfer across the val/test period shift (mechanism in Appendix~\ref{app:negative}). Repairable lag is therefore bias that is context-unpredictable and carried by a near-optimal backbone; misspecified fits remain open.

\paragraph{Where the repair does not help.}

Per-window gating of $\alpha$ by predicted change-point probability does not improve over the static weighting, and a randomly initialized gate matches it (Appendix~\ref{app:negative}).
