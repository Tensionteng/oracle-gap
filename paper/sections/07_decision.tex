\section{When Smoothness Is Rational: Descriptor-Driven Selective Prediction}
\label{sec:decision}

\subsection{A free auxiliary signal}

Where the oracle gap is non-positive, over-smoothing cannot be repaired, but the model can still report when its forecast is likely to degrade. We attach a shallow head (21K parameters, under 1\% of the backbone's parameter count) to the last hidden state of the forecaster, and train it jointly to predict a set of structural descriptors of the future window: the probability and position of an imminent change point, ordinal bins of drift, volatility, and trend slope, and a five-dimensional spectral summary. All labels are computed from the future window at training time and cost no annotation.

\paragraph{Descriptor supervision does not repair point accuracy at small scale.}

The head improves point MSE only marginally and only in specific cells: at best about one percent on PatchTST on ETTm1, with no reliable gain elsewhere and a negative effect when fine-tuning a 48M foundation model (mechanism analysis in Appendix~\ref{app:negative}). Most windows in these datasets are in the rational-shrinkage regime, so representation shaping has little bias to remove.

\paragraph{The descriptor output is a reliable forward-looking risk signal.}

Although the head does not improve the forecast, its change-point output predicts the forecast's error: abstaining on the top quintile by predicted change-point probability reduces the remaining MSE by 2.7\% on ETTh1 and 5.3\% on ETTm1 (Figure~\ref{fig:riskcov}). The signal outperforms random ranking ($+0.2\%$), context-volatility ranking ($+0.4\%$ and $+1.2\%$, actively harmful), and ranking by the true change-point label computed from the realized future ($-1.6\%$ and $-4.5\%$); against trained error-prediction baselines (ridge and MLP on context statistics, fit on the validation split) it wins on ETTh1 (at best $-0.3\%$) and ties on ETTm1, while requiring no validation data and no additional training. The head's smoothed probabilities rank windows more reliably than the noisy realized labels, suggesting a generalizable structural signal rather than memorized targets.

The same signal transfers to a fine-tuned foundation model at no extra cost: on Chronos-Bolt, abstaining by predicted change-point probability reduces the remaining MSE by 3.6\% on ETTh1 and 9.6\% on ETTm1---the latter exceeding the oracle-volatility bound ($-7.5\%$)---while context-volatility ranking is again ineffective ($+1.3\%$). Better backbones make the free descriptor signal more useful, not less.

\paragraph{Where the signal pays off.}
Extending the probe beyond ETT (Table~\ref{tab:decision}, Appendix~\ref{app:matrix}) shows the head's decision value is itself measurable from context statistics. Its best channel reduces the remaining MSE by $3.7$--$5.3\%$ on Traffic and Electricity, but its margin over the free context-volatility ranking is decisive only where that baseline fails (Traffic: $+3.2\%$), and vanishes where the lookback window already exposes the risk (Electricity $-5.3\%$ versus $-5.4\%$; weather $-4.6\%$ versus $-13.9\%$). Deploy the head where risk is forward-looking; where it is context-visible, the free baseline suffices.
