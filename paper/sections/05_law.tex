\section{The Repair Decision: Diagnosing When Anti-Smoothing Helps}
\label{sec:law}

The oracle gap is one branch of a repair decision; Section~\ref{sec:regionfocal} completes the tree with the level-lag regime. Cells are routed by the residual's dominant bias type, measured by the residual mean over its standard deviation: dispersion-dominated cells are gated by the oracle gap here, while lag-dominated cells are analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:regionfocal}. We first establish that the dispersion branch is a reliable gate. We evaluate the pairing of the oracle gap with \rfc~($\alpha{=}1$, Section~\ref{sec:regionfocal}) on fifteen dispersion-regime dataset-backbone cells, ten across datasets in Table~\ref{tab:law} and five across backbones in Appendix~\ref{app:matrix}. The sign of the gap predicts the direction of the intervention's effect on every cell, counting changes within $\pm 0.5\%$ as neutral. The two largest gaps give the largest improvements, and all non-positive gaps give neutral or negative effects; the magnitude relation is monotone but coarse, and we treat the sign, not the magnitude, as the diagnostic's decision variable. Under a sign-symmetric null the probability of observing zero contradictions across these cells is below $10^{-4}$, so the gate is a statistical regularity, not a coincidence.

The same law holds across five backbone families on Electricity (Appendix~\ref{app:matrix}, Table~\ref{tab:backbone}): the per-backbone gap, not the architecture family, orders the outcomes. The single positive-gap cell in which \rfc~does not help is FreTS on Traffic ($+0.9 \pm 0.1\%$ across three seeds against a gap of $+0.013$); its residual is level-lag dominated (mean over std $+0.28$, against $\le 0.01$ for both Traffic cells in Table~\ref{tab:law}), so the decision tree routes it to the lag branch, whose behavior on this cell is analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:regionfocal} and Appendix~\ref{app:negative}.

\subsection{A 665-cell audit of foundation models}
\label{sec:tsfmmap}

We extend the audit to thirteen zero-shot time-series foundation models, Chronos-Bolt at four scales (8M, 21M, 48M, 205M), Chronos-2, Sundial, Moirai-1.1 at two scales, Moirai-2.0, TimeMoE at two scales, Timer, and TiRex, on fifty dataset variants from GIFT-Eval \citep{gifteval} under the tail protocol. \paragraph{The audit as a benchmark statement.}
The map quantifies benchmark saturation: all ETT variants are non-positive on every model we tested, so anti-smoothing research evaluated on ETT alone is measuring rational shrinkage, and the remaining exploitable structure concentrates in transit, energy, and event-driven series. The saturation reading is corroborated from the accuracy side: within each variant, a model's accuracy rank anti-correlates with its oracle gap (median Spearman $\rho = -0.49$ across fifty variants, Wilcoxon $p < 10^{-6}$), so the better a model fits a dataset, the less repairable structure its residuals retain. The two leading positive-gap datasets are the exceptions that make the reading useful: on m4\_weekly ($\rho = +0.87$) and bizitobs\_application ($\rho = +0.52$), even the most accurate models leave measurable structure behind, which is precisely where repair methods should be developed and evaluated. We release the full matrix and the diagnostic code.
